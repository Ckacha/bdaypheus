why do I need to link my account?

Some things a bot just isn't allowed to do in Slack. Linking lets Bdaypheus do them as you.

Creating a planning channel

When you pick "Create a new private channel for me" in /bdaypheus, the channel is created with your account. Slack makes whoever creates a channel its channel manager, so you can rename it, archive it, and manage it yourself.

Inviting from a private channel

The bot can't see who's in a private channel it isn't in. With your account linked, it reads the member list of the channel you invite from using your access, so it knows who to invite. Once you're linked it does this for every channel you pick, public or private.

Why can't the bot just make the channel?

It could, but then the bot would be the channel manager, not you, and you couldn't rename or archive your own planning channel. Slack doesn't let regular bots hand that role to anyone else. The only way to set channel managers through the API is an admin-only tool that has to be installed across all of Hack Club by an org admin, which Bdaypheus isn't.

Why not just add the bot to my private channel?

You can! But adding a bot literally called Bdaypheus to your friends' private channel is a bit sus, and it'd see that channel forever. Linking means it only reads the member list when you run /bdaypheus.

Why Hack Club Auth?

Before Slack asks you to approve anything, you sign in with Hack Club Auth. That confirms the person linking is really you, so nobody can attach their own Slack token to your account.

How to link

  1. Run /link-bdaypheus in Slack, or press "Connect your Slack account" in the invite form.
  2. Sign in with Hack Club Auth.
  3. Approve the Slack permissions.
  4. Close the /bdaypheus form if it's open and run it again. It won't notice the new link until you reopen it.

Linked before channel creation existed? Run /link-bdaypheus and press "Reconnect anyway" to grant the new permission.

What exactly does it get?

The full list of permissions, what they're used for, and how to revoke them is on the warning page.
